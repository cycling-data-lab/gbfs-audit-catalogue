# Peer review — *Auditing GBFS bike-sharing feeds at country and global scale*

**Reviewer:** internal (Claude, peer-review skill) · **Date:** 2026-06-08
**Target venue:** Elsevier *Computer Standards & Interfaces*
**Recommendation:** Major revisions · **Scientific grade: 14 / 20**

## Summary
The paper audits the static `station_information` document of GBFS feeds at
two scales: an in-depth audit of 123 French systems and a descriptive sweep
of the 1,509-system MobilityData catalogue. It proposes a seven-class
anomaly taxonomy (A1–A7), a nine-step reproducible purging protocol, a
topology-aware A4 detector (HDBSCAN + spectral graph) that removes 8,005
centroid false positives, a LOOO consistency check, and a blind
dual-annotator human validation. A versioned FAIR dataset (46,307 stations)
ships with code, tests, Docker and a Zenodo DOI. The work is practically
valuable, unusually reproducible, and refreshingly honest about its limits;
scientific novelty is moderate (it is principally a careful data-quality /
standards-engineering contribution), and several validity claims are
over-stated relative to the evidence.

**Strengths**
- Best-in-class reproducibility: FAIR release, Croissant/DCAT manifests,
  pinned seeds, deterministic pipeline, unit tests + CI, Docker, public
  dashboard. This alone makes the artefact reusable and challengeable.
- The A4 v2 ablation is the strongest scientific result: a clean, falsifiable
  improvement over a legacy heuristic on anisotropic networks.
- Honesty: explicit limitations (L1–L5), negative controls, and a
  pre-registered falsifiable experiment roadmap.

**Weaknesses (load-bearing)**
- The "independent" ground truth is produced by the two co-authors.
- Per-rule precision/recall are computed on a pooled sample, which confounds
  the metrics; reported A3/A4/A5 precision is low and under-reconciled.
- The headline "100 % false positives" rests on a question (Q4) with κ=0.13
  and on 46 of 50 stations after dropping non-consensus cases.

## Major comments

**M1 — Ground truth is not independent of the authors.** The two "blind"
annotators are the lead author (urban-mobility researcher) and the co-author
(data engineer). Blinding to each other and to the pipeline mitigates
anchoring but does not remove the structural conflict: the gold standard
that validates the authors' own pipeline is generated by the authors. This
is the single biggest threat to Section 2.10 and to the abstract's framing.
*Action:* state this explicitly (not just "same institution"), and add at
least one genuinely external annotator on the decisive A4 discordant strata
(n=100), or down-grade the validation from "ground truth" to "expert
adjudication by the authors."

**M2 — Per-rule precision/recall are confounded by pooling.** The metrics
join each rule's flag against a factual predicate over the *entire* 325-row
sample, not over the population eligible for that rule. Because predicates
are shared (Q1 for A1, Q3 for A3/A6, Q4 for A4/A5), every non-bikeshare
station that is not A1-flagged becomes an A1 "false negative," inflating FN
(A1 FN=127) and depressing recall to 0.35 even though A1 precision is 0.97.
Symmetrically, A5 precision collapses to 0.08 partly because A5-flagged
stations from *other* strata enter the denominator. As written, the table
invites the reading "most rules have poor precision," which contradicts the
paper's thesis. *Action:* compute each rule's P/R within its eligible
stratum (or clearly define the negative-control pool per rule), and add one
paragraph reconciling the per-station table with the system-level argument.
Report A1's precision as the headline per-rule number.

**M3 — The A4 headline over-reaches.** "100 % of legacy-only flags are true
false positives (CI [92.3, 100]; n=46)" is computed on the 46 stations with
*consensus* Q4 out of 50, on a question whose global κ is 0.13. The
κ-paradox explanation (88 % raw agreement, skewed marginals) is legitimate,
but: (i) dropping the 4 non-consensus stations is a selection that should be
reported as a sensitivity bound (worst case 46/50 = 92 %); (ii) the abstract
states a flat "100 %," which is an over-statement of a bounded estimate.
*Action:* phrase as "≥92 % (46/46 consensus; 4 indeterminate)" and soften
the abstract.

**M4 — Reliability robustness of the rubric.** Q2 fails the protocol gate
(κ=0.17) due to a genuine annotator-interpretation mismatch (data-field
state vs physical plausibility), disclosed honestly. But this exposes a thin
calibration phase (5 joint stations) and raises the question of undetected
drift on Q3/Q4. *Action:* report the calibration-phase agreement, and state
which questions were re-aligned post-calibration.

**M5 — Generalisation claims exceed validated scope.** The title promises
"country and global scale," but the global layer (1,509 systems) is
incidence counting with no ground truth outside France; LOOO has K=7
(under-powered, acknowledged); the cross-country pilot is n=13 on a single
date and mostly negative controls. The framing of high LOOO CV as
"definitional, not overfitting" is plausible but convenient. *Action:*
either temper the title/abstract to "validated on France, profiled globally,"
or add external validation abroad.

**M6 — A degenerate confidence interval is reported as a result.** The
size-stratified cluster bootstrap on the 30.9 % removal rate yields
[-18.6 %, 63.6 %]; a 95 % interval spanning negative proportions is not
interpretable as a CI. The honesty is appreciated, but presenting it as an
"upper bound" muddies rather than clarifies. *Action:* replace with a
leave-the-giant-operator-out sensitivity (report the rate with/without
Vélib') and drop the degenerate interval, or move it to an appendix with a
clear "not a CI" caveat.

## Minor comments
- **m1.** Abstract is ~317 words; CSI limits abstracts to 250. Trim.
- **m2.** A2/A7 "precision = 1 by construction" is tautological — relabel as
  *definitional* (the rule reads the data), not as *validation*; human
  imagery cannot refute it, so it is not evidence of correctness.
- **m3.** A6 is untestable in this corpus (0 flags); make explicit in the
  per-rule table caption that A6 is excluded, so its absence is not read as
  recall = 0.
- **m4.** The "30.9 %" is alternately framed as a deterministic accounting
  fact and as an estimate with two CIs; pick one framing for the reader.
- **m5.** Define "true false positive" once, early — readers conflate the
  audit's FP with the detector's FP.
- **m6.** Sevici (0,0)-sentinel A5 artefact and the A4/A5 coupling
  recommendation are excellent; consider promoting to the main text design
  guidance rather than a footnote.
- **m7.** Figures could not be checked for integrity in this review; ensure
  all are vector PDF with colourblind-safe palettes and defined error bars.

## Questions for the authors
1. Were the two annotators the two authors? If so, why should the reader
   treat this as an independent ground truth?
2. What are the per-rule precision/recall when each rule is scored only on
   its eligible population (within-stratum)?
3. What are the 4 non-consensus A4 discordant-legacy stations, and how does
   the headline change if they are counted as false negatives?
4. Did any question other than Q2 require post-calibration re-alignment?
5. Can the global/LOOO scope be validated with even one external annotator
   or one non-French ground-truth city?

particular) are about *framing and analysis*, not new experiments, so the path from
14/20 to ~16–17/20 is short.
